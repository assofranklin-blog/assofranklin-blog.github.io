Is INSTAT Gabon’s Published Inflation Rate Reliable?

Gabon reported an official inflation rate of 1.3% in July 2026, while the cost of living remains a national priority. This review examines the transparency and compliance of the Harmonized Consumer Price Index (HCPI) against seven criteria, using public sources and regional comparisons.

FBy Franklin Assoumou-Ndong, statistics analystOttawa, Ontario, Canada · October 1, 2026
Independent analysis prepared with the support of artificial intelligence. The author takes full responsibility for its content after review.

The central finding

15 points out of 35. Nothing establishes that the figure is false. But nothing in the published material establishes that it is correct.

This finding concerns the public evidence available to verify the index; it is not an allegation of falsification.

Why ask this question?

Inflation is not just another statistic. The Bank of Central African States uses it to conduct monetary policy. It also informs adjustments to wages, pensions and contracts, the calculation of real growth, and assessments of purchasing power. A one-percentage-point error can flow through all of these decisions. It is therefore reasonable to ask whether the rate published by Gabon’s National Institute of Statistics (INSTAT Gabon) can be considered reliable.

In July 2026, the official rate stood at 1.3%. At the same time, tackling the high cost of living was a government priority, with price caps, a suspension of value-added tax on around 100 products, and the creation of a central purchasing agency. Both realities can coexist. The difficulty is that the published information does not let the public verify the figure.

A seven-criterion assessment

The analysis reviews INSTAT’s publications on the HCPI against seven criteria drawn from the international Consumer Price Index Manual and the harmonized guide of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC). It also compares Gabon’s arrangements with those of Statistics Canada, INSEE, Senegal’s ANSD and other CEMAC countries. The sources are public, cited and verifiable.

  1. Up-to-date weights: 1/5
  2. Coverage and price collection: 4/5
  3. Published processing methods: 2/5
  4. Documentation: 2/5
  5. Release calendar and timeliness: 1/5
  6. Data access: 2/5
  7. Revisions and accountability: 3/5

What the available evidence shows

The basket weights are based on a 2017 survey: by 2026, they were nine years old, whereas both the international standard and the CEMAC guide set a five-year limit. The regional guide calls for publication no later than the 10th of the following month. INSTAT instead gives a lead time of 30 to 45 days, and as of October 1, 2026, the August index had still not been published.

The available methodological note is one page long. It provides no detailed weights, calculation rules or revision policy. Older monthly bulletins are no longer accessible online. On the other hand, Gabon has the CEMAC’s densest price-collection network relative to its population, and in 2026 INSTAT published its first user-satisfaction survey.

Can subsidies explain low inflation?

Fuel subsidies and free public transport may help contain prices, but their effect cannot be established from the available data. The categories concerned would reduce inflation by only about 0.2 percentage points. Chad’s experience also shows why comparisons need care: inflation reached 5.7% in 2024 despite water and electricity being sold at a symbolic one franc, then fell to −2.6% in 2025 while housing and energy prices surged. The only available regional source also contradicts itself about energy in Gabon in two consecutive years: −1.7% in its text versus +2.3% in its table for 2024, then +1.0% versus +0.1% for 2025.

Fifteen recommendations to make the index verifiable

The report sets out 15 recommendations, 12 of which cost nothing: publish a release calendar, post the methodology and weights online, and release the data series in open formats. The most important recommendation is to rebase the index using the new household living-conditions survey, a field operation already being prepared. INSTAT’s own user-satisfaction survey also recommends publishing release calendars and meeting deadlines.

The gap is not in calculation, but in publication. It can be closed in less than two years.
