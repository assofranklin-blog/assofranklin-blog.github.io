Do subsidies explain low inflation?

The explanation is plausible, but unproven. The most direct test compares headline inflation with inflation excluding energy. Yet the only public source publishing these series for Gabon contradicts itself across two consecutive editions. Select a version.

Gabon’s 2024 energy index, according to the CEMAC Commission’s note

The 2025 note has the same discrepancy: energy rises 1.0% in the text and 0.1% in the table. Both values are below overall inflation (1.8%), but without published weights the size of any subsidy effect cannot be measured.

Other causes also matter, but have not been tested
  • Scale. The categories covered by the measures contribute about −0.2 percentage points: without them, 2024 inflation would be around 1.4%, far below CEMAC’s 4.1%.
  • Counterexample. Chad had 5.7% inflation in 2024 despite water and electricity costing a symbolic one franc; in 2025 housing rose 19.4% and energy 24.7%, yet inflation was −2.6%.
  • Monetary anchor. The CFA franc is pegged to the euro; prices of imported goods in Gabon fell 0.3% in 2024.
  • Global markets, demand and base effects. Falling world food prices, weak domestic demand and a high 2023 base (3.6%).
